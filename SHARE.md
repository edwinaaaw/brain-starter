# Brain Starter 0.2 Test Guide

Brain Starter turns “I know what to do, but I cannot start” into one real, observable ten-minute start. AI may take over mechanical resistance such as searching, sorting, splitting, or scaffolding; the user retains judgment, learning, authorship, and bodily consent.

## Install

Give Codex the complete plugin folder or archive and say:

> Install this local Brain Starter plugin as a separate plugin. Do not overwrite existing plugins. After installation, tell me how to invoke it in a new task.

After installation, open a new Codex task and describe the real block. You can also explicitly say “Use Brain Starter.”

## Test it on a real task

Choose something that exists today but has not started. Do not invent a task for the test.

Example prompts:

- “I know I need to write this, but I do not want to start.”
- “My head is crowded. I have two hours and low energy. Help me choose what to begin.”
- “I do not want to exercise tonight, but I want you to get me into the first safe movement.”
- “I was interrupted. Take me back to the last real step.”
- “Give me a short attention refuel, then move me into the actual task.”

Afterward, answer five questions:

1. Did it get you to a real first action within three minutes?
2. Which sentence or step helped most?
3. Which part felt annoying, preachy, complicated, or untrustworthy?
4. What visible result existed after ten minutes?
5. Would you use it the next time you get stuck? Why or why not?

## Current boundaries

- Included: text or voice-input adaptation, start cards, embedded attention warm-ups, re-entry after interruption, patterns for work, study, creation, exercise, and everyday tasks, privacy-minimal session logging, and text-based momentum feedback.
- Not included: a Forest-style visual Brain Station, account sync, leaderboards, streaks, or a standalone mobile app.
- Momentum rewards real action only. It does not reward chatting, planning, or time spent in Codex, and it never deducts points for missed days.
